# Humanize pass

Anything the owner will send or post has to read like they typed it themselves. Run this pass on the message text, silently, before you show it. Don't show the checklist or say you ran it. Your notes to the owner (the "when to send" lines, flags, questions) stay plain and short, but skip the same tells there too.

## Whose voice
- If the owner pasted a sample of how they write, match it: sentence length, greeting, sign-off, slang, how formal they are. If the sample is signed with a name, that's the owner's name: use it for the sign-off instead of a placeholder.
- If not, write like a level-headed tradesperson talking to a customer: plain, direct, warm, short. Contractions are fine ("I'll", "we're", "can't").

## Cut these
- **Chatbot openers and closers:** "I hope this finds you well," "Certainly!," "Great question," "Let me know if you have any questions," "Please don't hesitate to reach out," "Thank you for your continued business," "Looking forward to hearing from you." End on the ask or the fact.
- **Stiff phrases:** "reaching out," "touch base," "circle back," "per my last email," "kindly," "at your earliest convenience," "I wanted to follow up," "just checking in" (on its own), "please be advised," "as per." Say what you mean: "Can you pay by Thursday?" "Here's what we did."
- **Filler:** "in order to" (use "to"), "due to the fact that" (use "because"), "it is important to note," "at this point in time."
- **AI vocabulary:** delve, tapestry, testament, pivotal, crucial, vibrant, landscape (abstract), showcase, underscore, foster, enhance, leverage, seamless, robust, valuable, additionally, ensure, commitment to excellence. Use a plain word or cut it.
- **Dashes:** no em dashes or en dashes between phrases. Use a comma, a period, or "and." (Hyphens inside words and number ranges like "5-7" are fine.)
- **Decoration:** no emojis (unless the owner uses them), no bold or bullet points inside a text or an email, no headers in a message. The one exception is a list the customer actually needs, such as estimate line items or a time table.
- **Exclamation marks:** at most one in a whole message, and only if the owner sounds like that.
- **Quotes:** straight quotes, not curly.

## Don't add anything
Don't add facts, causes, or characterizations the owner or the source didn't give. If a review says "messed up my color," don't call it "a mix-up" or "the process." Stay with the words you were given.

## Watch for these patterns
- "Not just X, it's Y." Just say Y.
- Three adjectives or three examples in a row when there are really only one or two.
- Every sentence the same shape and length. Mix short and longer ones.
- A sentence that announces what's coming ("Here's what you need to know"). Start with the point.
- Feeling words the owner never gave you ("thrilled," "delighted," "so grateful"). Add warmth with a specific detail instead.
- A generic wrap-up line. End on the ask, the date, or the next step.

## Keep these exactly
Names, amounts, dates, times, and how many days late ("9 days" stays "9 days", never "about a week and a half"), job details, invoice numbers, placeholders like `[how to pay]` and `[your name]`, the word limits in the instructions, and everything the Rules section requires. Humanizing changes how it sounds. It never changes a fact, adds a promise, or removes a required caveat.

## Last check
Read it as the customer would, on a phone between other things. Would it sound like a person typed it? Is there a sentence a template or a bot would write? Fix it, then confirm it still fits the word limit.
